GAIHF — Generative AI Hardening Framework

Computer science degree final-year thesis · Sole author

AI assistants built into workplace tools, such as Microsoft 365 Copilot or Google Gemini, can access emails, documents, and meetings across an entire organization. That makes them very useful, but it also creates a risk: what if someone tricks the assistant into revealing confidential information they shouldn't have access to? In my Bachelor's Thesis in Computer Engineering (University of Granada), I study this problem using a technique called red teaming, which means attacking a system in a controlled way to uncover its weaknesses before a real attacker does.

As a result, I developed GAIHF (Generative AI Hardening Framework), a Python tool that automates the process: it takes the results of the attack tests, identifies the security flaws, scores them by severity, and proposes concrete measures to fix them. It also generates clear reports for both technical and management teams, and shows how security improves over time. Its recommendations are based on recognized industry standards (CIS Controls, MITRE ATLAS, and the OWASP Top 10 for LLMs), so any company can use them as a guide. For this project I worked with Python, Azure OpenAI, PyRIT (Microsoft's framework for AI security testing), and Jupyter Notebooks, and the tool includes a complete test suite to ensure its reliability.

AssistanceSupport

Higher technical degree final proyect

AssistanceSupport is a system designed for advanced academic settings such as upper secondary school (bachillerato) or university. Its goal is to eliminate the process teachers have to carry out to verify students' physical attendance, commonly known as "taking attendance" or "calling the roll."

It would work as follows: the student enters the classroom and, through their AssistanceSupport app account, connects via the Bluetooth protocol (IEEE 802.15) to the Raspberry Pi, sending the student's data to it over that protocol. The Raspberry Pi, which is always connected to a server written in Python that in turn connects to a database, would then record in that database, with the exact date and time, the student who has entered the class. In this way, the student's attendance is registered for a specific classroom, a specific subject, and a specific date and time.
